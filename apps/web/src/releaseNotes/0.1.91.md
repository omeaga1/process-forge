# A crystalliser that actually crystallises

- **Yield now comes from solubility.** The batch crystalliser used to send a typed-in 65% to the liquor port whatever you did. Now it works out how much solute the cooled liquor can keep dissolved and crystallises the rest. Cool further, or feed it stronger brine, and you get more crystals. Cool less and you get fewer.
- **Checked against published data.** A 30% magnesium sulfate feed cooled to 15 °C gives 41.5% of the solute as Epsom salt crystals, and the mother liquor leaves saturated at 23.5%. Both match a hand calculation from the CRC solubility table.
- **Crystals stay behind when the liquor is decanted.** The slurry port carries the crystals (and a little liquor, set by "Crystals in the settled slurry"), so the centrifuge and dryer downstream have real solids to work on.
- **New for Claude and for new unit designs:** `solubility()` and `crystal_yield()` for magnesium sulfate, potassium nitrate, potassium chloride and sodium chloride, and a "retain" option on a batch drain step.
- An existing crystalliser on a saved flowsheet keeps its old fixed split until you replace it with the new one from the palette, and the feed tank needs its composition (water and MgSO4).
